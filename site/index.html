      Mean radiant temperature assumption

      The Physics roof station does not measure mean radiant temperature, which is one of the inputs needed to calculate UTCI. For both models, this page assumes mean radiant temperature equals the station's air temperature.

      UTCI categories and guidance

      These are general descriptions of possible responses and precautions for each UTCI range. Individual risk varies; follow local weather and health advice, especially during extreme conditions.

      Thermal-stress category UTCI range (°C) Possible physiological response General recommendation
      Extreme heat stress > +46 Overheating and serious heat illness may develop. Avoid outdoor activity; seek a cool indoor place, reduce exertion, and drink fluids.
      Very strong heat stress +38 to +46 Heavy sweating and rising body heat are possible. Limit activity, take regular breaks in shade or air conditioning, and drink fluids.
      Strong heat stress +32 to < +38 Sweating and increasing body heat may occur during prolonged exposure. Reduce exertion, drink fluids, and use shaded, well-ventilated places.
      Moderate heat stress +26 to < +32 Warm skin and some sweating are possible. Keep water available and take breaks in shade if needed.
      No thermal stress +9 to < +26 Conditions are generally thermally comfortable. No special precautions indicated by this category.
      Slight cold stress 0 to < +9 Hands may begin to feel cold. Consider warm layers, gloves, and a hat for time outdoors.
      Moderate cold stress −13 to < 0 Chilling and discomfort of exposed skin may occur. Cover exposed skin, insulate hands and feet, and keep moving if comfortable.
      Strong cold stress −27 to < −13 Chills and facial discomfort may progress to numbness with exposure. Cover the face and extremities, wear warm layers, and limit time outside.
      Very strong cold stress −40 to < −27 Pain or numbness and frostbite risk increase in prolonged exposure. Stay sheltered where possible; if outside, cover skin and keep exposure brief.
      Extreme cold stress < −40 Frostbite and other serious cold injury can develop rapidly. Avoid going outside if possible and follow official cold-weather advice.

      Guidance adapted from Błażejczyk K. et al. (2010), “UTCI - a new index for assessing human thermal loads,” Przegląd Geograficzny, 82(1), 49–71.

      Source: Oxford Physics roof weather station. UTCI is an estimate based on the stated inputs and mean radiant temperature assumption.
